The NoorSoul Philosophy · نور

Bringing 1,400 Years of Islamic Spiritual Wisdom Into Your Pocket

Named after the Quranic concept of divine light (نور), NoorSoul is designed to illuminate daily worship, contemplation, and self-understanding — never to judge, predict the unseen, or replace authentic faith.

Why NoorSoul Exists

Every Soul Carries a Unique Spiritual Rhythm

Modern wellness apps rarely reflect Islamic spirituality, while authentic classical traditions — Hisab al-Jummal (Abjad), Ilm al-Jawahir, daily Wird, Muraqaba, and prophetic Ruqyah — remain scattered across centuries-old Arabic manuscripts.

Founded by Faisal Abdul Mateen Wasif, NoorSoul begins with your name to compute your personal Abjad profile and unfolds a calm, deeply structured spiritual companion built around the five daily prayers and the Hijri calendar.

Ethical Boundaries

Tafakkur · Not Divination · Not Magic

“NoorSoul is a tool for tafakkur — deep Islamic reflection. It is not divination, not magic, and not a substitute for scholarly guidance.”

Islamic orthodoxy draws a crystal-clear line between Tafakkur (contemplative reflection and seeking closeness to Allah through His 99 Names and Quranic words) and forbidden practices such as fortune-telling or sorcery.

NoorSoul never claims to predict future events, override Allah's decree (Qadr), or replace medical care. High-caution traditional practices are strictly supervised or referred to verified scholars via Murshid Connect.

Our Knowledge Corpus

Rooted in Classical Islamic Scholarship

Every calculation, crystal citation, dhikr prescription, and Ruqyah recitation in the NoorSoul app maps directly to verified historical works.

Ibn Sina — Al-Qanun fi al-Tibb

11th-century Canon of Medicine documenting the physical and temperamental properties of natural gemstones and minerals.

Al-Biruni — Kitab al-Jamahir

11th-century Book of Precious Stones establishing classical Islamic gemstone classification, origin, and therapeutic context.

Imam Al-Ghazali — Ihya Ulum al-Din

11th-century revival of religious sciences guiding the inner etiquette of the 99 Divine Names and heart purification.

Ibn Arabi — Al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya

13th-century cosmological framework mapping Arabic letters, classical elements, and the spiritual stations of the soul.

Al-Buni — Shams al-Maarif al-Kubra

13th-century encyclopaedia of Ilm al-Huruf, Abjad numerical correspondences, and the mathematical Wafq tradition.

Sahih al-Bukhari & Sahih Muslim

9th-century canonical Prophetic traditions providing every authenticated Ruqyah verse, healing du'a, and daily dhikr foundation.
